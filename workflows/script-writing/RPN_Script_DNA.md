# RPN Script DNA

Use this file as a style reference for `Script Writer`.

The goal is **not** to copy RPN word-for-word. The goal is to internalize the deeper structure behind how his short-form scripts work, then apply that structure to new topics accurately and creatively.

## What To Emulate

- **Claim-first openings**
  - Start with a strong statement, observation, or implication.
  - Avoid warm-up lines, slow setup, or generic background.

- **Stakes by line 2**
  - By the second line, introduce consequence, urgency, implication, contradiction, or tension.
  - The viewer should immediately understand why they need to keep watching.

- **Bridge lines**
  - Use short transition lines that create motion.
  - Examples:
    - `Here's how it works.`
    - `Now let me show you why.`
    - `So what does that mean?`
    - `Here's the important part.`

- **Proof beats**
  - The body should not feel like a list of facts.
  - Move through examples, demos, use cases, comparisons, or steps.
  - Show why the claim matters through proof.

- **Translation into the viewer's world**
  - Regularly connect the idea to what it means for:
    - creators
    - business owners
    - operators
    - the specific audience implied by the brief

- **Zoom-out endings**
  - End with either:
    - a practical takeaway
    - a perspective shift
    - a market implication
    - a bigger future-facing point

- **Creator-native spoken pacing**
  - Short lines
  - Clean phrasing
  - High signal density
  - Easy to say out loud

- **Visual-support thinking**
  - Suggest B-roll, screen recordings, pattern interrupts, or on-screen text when useful.
  - Visuals should support the spoken beat, not act as filler.

## Core Structure

Default structural model:

`claim -> stakes/implication -> bridge -> proof beats -> zoom-out meaning -> optional CTA`

This is more accurate than a simple `hook -> explain -> CTA` model.

## What To Avoid

- Do not copy exact phrases unless the topic naturally earns them.
- Do not overuse hype words like:
  - `insane`
  - `crazy`
  - `game-changer`
  - `nobody is talking about this`
- Do not force a dramatic market conclusion unless the evidence truly supports it.
- Do not say things like:
  - `X is cooked`
  - `X killer`
  - `this destroys the category`
  unless the source clearly earns that level of certainty.
- Do not turn every script into the same cadence or sentence rhythm.
- Do not copy his exact CTA patterns mechanically.

## Claim Escalation Rule

Always aim for the strongest **defensible** framing, not just the strongest possible wording.

Use this rough ladder:

1. `This is worth paying attention to.`
2. `This puts pressure on the current workflow or category.`
3. `This could change how this category works.`
4. `This may become a serious threat to incumbents.`
5. Only use stronger market-destruction framing if the source clearly supports it.

## Source Discipline

When working from transcripts, articles, videos, or research:

- separate grounded fact from inference
- narrow claims when the evidence is thin
- use implication when certainty is not earned
- do not invent details from inaccessible links or partial context

## Desired Output Feel

The ideal output should feel:

- sharp
- creator-native
- high-momentum
- strategically framed
- useful
- visually intuitive
- grounded
